Many people reach their late twenties or thirties with a bachelor's degree and a growing wish to work in healthcare. Starting over with a second four-year degree feels impossible, and for most careers it isn't necessary.

A number of programs are designed specifically for people who already have a degree. Here are the fastest routes, grouped by how quickly you can start working.

## Start now: jobs that hire with your current degree

- **Clinical research coordinator.** Coordinators help run clinical trials at hospitals, universities, and research sites. A health, science, or social science degree is usually enough to start. [See the coordinator route](/#career-crc).
- **Research technician.** If you have a science degree, academic and biotech labs hire research techs directly. [See the research tech route](/#career-researchtech).

These jobs also give you healthcare experience, which strengthens applications to graduate programs later.

## About 12 months

- **Medical laboratory scientist (MLS).** For biology and chemistry graduates, a 12-month post-baccalaureate program prepares you for the ASCP certification exam. It's one of the quickest routes into a certified, in-demand clinical career. [See the MLS route](/#career-mls).
- **Nuclear medicine technologist.** Certificate programs for degree holders often take around a year. Check that the program is JRCNMT-accredited. [See the nuclear medicine route](/#career-nucmed).

## About 12 to 18 months

- **Registered nurse through an accelerated BSN.** Second-degree nursing programs accept a bachelor's in any field and typically take 12 to 18 months, followed by the NCLEX-RN. You'll need prerequisites such as anatomy, physiology, and microbiology first. [See the nursing route](/#career-rn).
- **Diagnostic medical sonographer.** Certificate programs for people with prior degrees commonly take 12 to 18 months. Choose a CAAHEP-accredited program so you can sit for registry exams. [See the sonography route](/#career-sonography).

## About 12 to 24 months

- **Cytotechnologist.** Certificate or master's programs for science graduates who enjoy microscope work and cancer diagnosis. [See the cytotechnology route](/#career-cytotech).

## Before you enroll, check three things

1. **Accreditation.** Graduates of non-accredited programs often can't sit for the certification exam that employers require. Each career page links to the right accreditor.
2. **Prerequisites.** Many accelerated programs require recent science courses. Budget a semester or two to finish them if needed.
3. **Clinical placements.** Ask how the program arranges clinical rotations. Strong programs place you; weaker ones leave you to find your own.

## Want a longer view?

If you're open to two or three more years of school, the options expand to physician assistant, occupational and physical therapy, genetic counseling, and more.
