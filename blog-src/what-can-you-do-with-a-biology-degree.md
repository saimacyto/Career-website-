If you finished a biology degree and medical school isn't your plan, or isn't your plan anymore, you are far from stuck. Biology is one of the most flexible starting points in healthcare. Your coursework in cell biology, chemistry, genetics, and anatomy already covers most of the prerequisites that health programs ask for.

The hard part is usually not a lack of options. It's that nobody shows you the full list. Here it is, grouped by how long each path takes after your bachelor's.

## Careers you can start with the degree you have

These roles hire biology graduates directly. They pay while you decide on your long-term path, and the experience makes later applications stronger.

- **Research technician.** Run experiments in university, hospital, or biotech labs. It's the most common first job for new biology graduates. [See the research tech route](/#career-researchtech).
- **Clinical research coordinator.** Help run clinical trials: enroll patients, track data, and keep studies compliant. A strong fit if you like people and organization. [See the coordinator route](/#career-crc).
- **Regulatory affairs associate.** Prepare the documents that get new drugs and devices approved. Detail-oriented, office-based work in industry.

## Careers that take about one to two more years

These programs are built for people who already hold a bachelor's degree, and a biology degree is exactly what most of them look for.

- **Medical laboratory scientist (MLS).** A 12-month post-baccalaureate certificate at a NAACLS-accredited program prepares you for the ASCP Board of Certification exam. You'd run the tests behind blood counts, infections, transfusions, and cancer diagnoses. [See the MLS route](/#career-mls).
- **Cytotechnologist.** Screen cells under the microscope to detect cancer and pre-cancer. Certificate and master's programs take roughly 12 to 24 months. [See the cytotechnology route](/#career-cytotech).
- **Registered nurse through an accelerated BSN.** Second-degree nursing programs typically run 12 to 18 months, then you take the NCLEX-RN. [See the nursing route](/#career-rn).
- **Diagnostic medical sonographer or nuclear medicine technologist.** Many imaging programs offer certificate tracks of about a year or more for degree holders.

## Careers that take two to four more years

These are graduate programs where your biology prerequisites give you a head start.

- **Physician assistant (PA).** Master's programs usually run about 27 months. PAs diagnose, treat, and prescribe, and can move between specialties during their careers. Most programs want direct patient-care hours before you apply. [See the PA route](/#career-pa).
- **Genetic counselor.** A two-year master's that combines genetics with counseling. You help patients and families understand genetic test results and risk. [See the genetic counselor route](/#career-gc).
- **Pharmacist.** A four-year PharmD after prerequisites, then the NAPLEX exam. [See the pharmacist route](/#career-pharmacist).
- **Occupational or physical therapist.** OT master's and doctoral programs and the three-year DPT both accept biology graduates once prerequisites are complete.
- **Public health professional.** An MPH typically takes one to two years and leads to roles in epidemiology, infection prevention, and health programs.

## Careers that take four or more years

- **Dentist** (DDS or DMD, four years) and **physician** (MD or DO, four years plus residency) remain options. So does a **PhD in the biomedical sciences** if you love research.

## How to choose between them

Start with three questions:

1. **How much patient contact do you want?** Lab and research roles are mostly behind the scenes. PA, nursing, and therapy are hands-on all day.
2. **How many more years of school are you ready for?** The list above runs from zero to eight or more.
3. **What does a normal day look like?** Shadow someone in the two or three careers that interest you most. A single day of shadowing tells you more than weeks of reading.

Then check that any program you consider is accredited. Only graduates of accredited programs can sit for most certification exams, and each career page on Healthcare Tracks links to the right accreditor.

If you're still unsure, the [five-question quiz](/#quiz) suggests three careers to research based on what you enjoy. You can also [start from your degree](/#degree) to see paths people with a science background commonly take.
