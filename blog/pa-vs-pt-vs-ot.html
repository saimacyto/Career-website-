PA vs. PT vs. OT: How to Choose Between Three Popular Health Careers

Physician assistant, physical therapist, and occupational therapist programs attract many of the same students. Compare the work, training length, degrees, and exams side by side.

By Saima Ahmad, MPH
September 26, 2026 · 2 min read

  1. The short version
  2. Side by side
  3. How the work differs
  4. What programs look for
  5. How to decide

Physician assistant (PA), physical therapist (PT), and occupational therapist (OT) are three of the most popular goals for students with health, biology, kinesiology, and psychology degrees. They're all hands-on, patient-centered, graduate-level careers, but the day-to-day work is quite different.

The short version

  • PA: You diagnose and treat illness, order tests, and prescribe medication as part of a medical team.
  • PT: You help people move better and recover from injury, surgery, or chronic conditions.
  • OT: You help people do the everyday activities that matter to them, such as dressing, working, cooking, and learning, after illness, injury, or disability.

How the work differs

PAs work in almost every medical specialty and can change specialties during their careers without going back to school. The pace is often fast, especially in emergency medicine and surgery. If you're drawn to diagnosis and medicine but don't want the length of medical school, PA is worth considering.

PTs focus on the body's movement system. A typical day involves assessing strength and range of motion, designing exercise programs, and working hands-on with patients recovering from surgery, strokes, or sports injuries.

OTs look at the whole person and their environment. An OT might help a stroke survivor relearn to cook, adapt a classroom for a child with autism, or support someone managing a mental health condition. If you like creative problem-solving and psychology, OT often fits.

What programs look for

All three are competitive. Most programs expect:

  • A bachelor's degree, in any major, with the program's prerequisites completed
  • Strong grades, especially in science courses
  • Healthcare experience. PA programs often require direct patient-care hours; PT and OT programs usually ask for observation hours with licensed therapists.
  • Letters of recommendation and a personal statement

Requirements vary by program, so check each school's website early.

How to decide

  1. Shadow all three. Spend a day with a PA, a PT, and an OT. Most people know quickly which one feels right.
  2. Think about the problems you want to solve. Illness and diagnosis point to PA. Movement and recovery point to PT. Independence and daily life point to OT.
  3. Consider time and cost. Program lengths differ, so compare tuition and time out of the workforce.
