    Before you apply

    Four questions that save time and money

    Ask these about every career on your shortlist before you commit to a program.

    1. What does the workday look like?Compare patient contact, lab time, how much you're on your feet, and the schedule. Shadow someone if you can.
    2. Which degree actually qualifies you?Your degree may cover some prerequisites, but licensed roles usually need a specific professional program.
    3. Is the program accredited?Only graduates of accredited programs can sit for most certification exams. Use the directory on each career page.
    4. What does your state require?Check your state licensing board for exams, fees, and rules before you enroll.

    Choosing a program

    Where to study, how to apply, and what to compare

    The school you choose shapes your cost, your clinical training, and whether you can sit for your exam. Use these tools before you apply anywhere.

    Accreditor directories are the official list of programs whose graduates can sit for the certification exam. If a school isn't listed, ask why before you enroll.

    Look up each item on the program's website, or ask at an information session. Many accreditors require programs to publish their exam pass, graduation, and employment rates.

    No accredited program is "easy," and it shouldn't be. But some programs have fewer barriers to getting in. Look for these features:

    • Rolling admissionApplications are reviewed as they arrive, so applying early improves your odds.
    • No GRE requiredMany MPH, OT, PA, and nursing programs have dropped the GRE.
    • Direct applicationsPrograms outside the big application services often get fewer applicants.
    • Community college and hospital-based programsMLT, radiography, sonography, respiratory therapy, PTA, OTA, and ADN nursing programs offer lower-cost, local routes with strong clinical training.
    • Post-bacc and accelerated tracksPost-bacc MLS certificates and accelerated BSN programs are built for people who already have a degree.
    • Bridge programsMLT-to-MLS, LPN-to-RN, ADN-to-BSN, and PTA-to-DPT routes let you work while you move up.
    • Part-time, evening, and hybrid formatsThese programs suit career changers and often draw a different applicant pool.
    • Multiple start datesPrograms that start more than once a year give you more chances to apply.
    • Newer programsNew programs may have more open seats. Confirm their accreditation status first, and that graduates can sit for the exam.
    Before you apply anywhere: an easier admission never makes up for a program that isn't accredited. If you can't sit for the certification exam, you can't work in the field.

    Start with your own state

    Public universities and community colleges charge much less to state residents, and many public health programs, especially medical and dental schools, give residents priority for seats.

    Check residency rules early. Most states require you to live there for about a year before you qualify for in-state tuition.

    Regional tuition exchanges

    These programs let students from member states pay reduced tuition at public schools in other member states. Health programs are often included, but check each program's participation.

    • WICHEWestern states (WUE, WRGP, and the PSEP health-professions program)
    • SREB Academic Common MarketSouthern states
    • MSEPMidwestern states
    • NEBHE Tuition BreakNew England states

    Check state licensing

    Licensing rules differ by state. Before you choose a program, confirm it qualifies you for a license where you want to work, especially for online programs based in another state.

    • NCSBNState boards of nursing
    • FSBPTPhysical therapy licensing
    • NABNursing home administrators

    Lower the cost

    Federal and state programs repay loans or offer scholarships for people who work in underserved areas or at nonprofit employers.

    • FAFSA / Federal Student AidGrants and loans
    • National Health Service CorpsScholarships and loan repayment
    • Nurse CorpsNursing scholarships and loan repayment
    • Public Service Loan ForgivenessNonprofit and public employers
    • College ScorecardCompare cost and outcomes

        Saima Ahmad is a healthcare leader with more than 25 years of experience in clinical laboratory operations, anatomic pathology, quality improvement, and team development. She holds a Master of Public Health and is completing her doctoral studies.

        Throughout her career, Saima has helped healthcare professionals build their skills, find new opportunities, and make important career decisions. She created Healthcare Tracks to make that same guidance available to anyone who wants a career in healthcare but isn't sure where to start, whatever their degree.

        Through clear career pathways, practical resources, and real-world insight, Saima helps visitors explore professions such as medical laboratory science, physical and occupational therapy, nursing, physician assistant, public health, healthcare administration, and many more. Her mission is simple: to help every person find a healthcare career that fits their strengths, interests, and goals.

        The story behind Healthcare Tracks

        Over 25 years in clinical laboratories and healthcare teams, I kept hearing the same question: "What can I actually do with my degree?"

        It came from biology graduates, but just as often from people with degrees in social work, public health, health science, chemistry, or physics, and from pre-med students whose plans had changed. Many of them had real talent and no map.

        Healthcare Tracks is that map: every path laid out plainly, with the honest details I'd share with a colleague who asked me for advice.

        Saima Ahmad
